# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Primary audience is recruiters and hiring managers evaluating the developer's work as a portfolio piece, alongside the developer themselves using it as a hands-on DevOps learning vehicle. Secondary: anyone spinning up the project to explore the polyglot microservices architecture. The user is technically literate, expects polish, and will judge the UI as evidence of craft.

## Product Purpose

CollabSphere is a distributed social networking platform built across six polyglot microservices (Java, Python, Go, Node.js), three databases (PostgreSQL, Neo4j, MongoDB), and Kafka. On the surface it's a professional network with feeds, connections, direct messages, community Spheres, and notifications. Under the hood it's a DevOps learning vehicle demonstrating service decomposition, async messaging, and polyglot persistence. Success looks like a portfolio-ready product that communicates both engineering depth and design intention at a glance.

## Brand Personality

Warm, expressive, community-driven. Not corporate, not enterprise, not another LinkedIn clone. Feels handcrafted and alive — closer to Dribbble-meets-Discord than SaaS-template-blue. Voice is direct and human; the UI feels like it was made by someone who cares, not generated.

## Anti-references

- **Generic SaaS dashboard**: Inter everywhere, purple-to-blue gradients, identical cards nested in cards, gray text on white, eyebrows on every section heading, numbered scaffolding. The dominant AI design template — avoid in every dimension.
- **LinkedIn (corporate blue)**: Stiff, navy/white enterprise UI with zero visual personality.

## Design Principles

1. **Community over corporate** — every surface should feel like it belongs to real people, not an org chart. Spheres should feel like rooms, not feature tabs.
2. **Warmth through craft** — personality is carried by deliberate color choices, expressive typography, and purposeful motion, not by decorative widgets and icon tiles.
3. **Show the work** — as a DevOps showcase, the UI itself is evidence of craft. Every detail (spacing, contrast, transition timing) signals intent.
4. **Distinct, not derivative** — visual decisions should be unguessable from the category alone. A recruiter should not be able to confuse this with a bootstrapped LinkedIn clone.
5. **Alive, not exhausting** — energy comes from hierarchy, motion, and color contrast — not from noise, borders, or busyness.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA. Body text ≥4.5:1 contrast, large/bold text ≥3:1, keyboard-navigable, visible focus ring on all interactive elements, reduced-motion alternative for all animations.
